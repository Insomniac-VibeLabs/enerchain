# Interplanetary economics

Edition: doc-0.4 (2026-10-08).

## Plain language

Regions trade the token, not the electrons. A settlement on the Moon, on Mars, in orbit, or in another system is another region. The token is proof that generation was measured there. It is not a physical export of electricity. A watt-hour is a watt-hour in every region. The local price does not have to match.

## From feeder to system

Terrestrial regions already clear different prices for the same physical megawatt-hour [Schweppe et al. 1988; Hogan 1992]. Enerchain extends the region list:

- interconnected terrestrial grids
- a planetary book
- an orbital book
- a solar-system book

Each book mints from local generation under the same hardware schedule. Holders trade the unit across books. The conversion is public. The protocol does not force the prices to be equal. A new settlement with thin generation can clear a higher price than a mature grid with surplus, which is the incentive to build generation there. Off-Earth industry is constrained by local energy [Metzger et al. 2013]. That scarcity is why the unit is the trade good.

## Transfer across distance

Account transfer is the same operation everywhere: a signature over amount, destination, and nonce, on a public ledger. Cross-region settlement uses the delay the path actually has. Delay-tolerant networking is the published approach for interplanetary links [Burleigh et al. 2003]. Local books finalize locally. A transfer between books carries the signed unit and completes when the message arrives.

## Vision

A multi-planetary species trades in an electricity unit the way interconnected grids trade power now. Measurement is shared. Price is regional. The coin moves.
